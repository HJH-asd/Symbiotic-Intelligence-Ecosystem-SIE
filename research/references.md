# Research References / 研究参考文献

> Curated starting bibliography. Source findings must not be confused with SIE-specific predictions. / 初始参考文献目录。不得将原论文结论等同于SIE架构已获验证。

## R01 — Leibo et al. (2017)
**Multi-agent Reinforcement Learning in Sequential Social Dilemmas**
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.03037
- Supported finding: Sequential multi-agent dilemmas show learned cooperation/conflict can vary with environmental conditions including resource abundance.
- Boundary: Does not establish a universal threshold or prove SIE.

## R02 — Nowak (2006)
**Five Rules for the Evolution of Cooperation**
- Link: https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1133755
- Supported finding: Describes five classes of mechanisms supporting cooperation under specified evolutionary models.
- Boundary: Not a prescription for AI governance.

## R03 — Ostrom (1990)
**Governing the Commons: The Evolution of Institutions for Collective Action**
- Link: https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511807763
- Supported finding: Case-based institutional analysis of self-governance of common-pool resources.
- Boundary: Transfer to digital agents and robotics needs separate validation.

## R04 — Turner et al. (2021)
**Optimal Policies Tend to Seek Power**
- Link: https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/file/c26820b8a4c1b3c2aa868d6d57e14a79-Paper.pdf
- Supported finding: Formal results for power-seeking tendencies under particular MDP assumptions.
- Boundary: Does not establish that all real-world AIs seek power or self-replicate.

## R05 — Woods (2015)
**Four Concepts for Resilience and the Implications for the Future of Resilience Engineering**
- Link: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ress.2015.03.018
- Supported finding: Distinguishes rebound, robustness, graceful extensibility, and sustained adaptive capacity.
- Boundary: Does not empirically validate the proposed SIE architecture.

## R06 — Perolat et al. (2017)
**A multi-agent reinforcement learning model of common-pool resource appropriation**
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06600
- Supported finding: Multi-agent simulation of common-pool resource dilemmas and dynamics of exclusion, sustainability, and inequality.
- Boundary: Results are environment-specific.

## R07 — Ohtsuki et al. (2006)
**A simple rule for the evolution of cooperation on graphs and social networks**
- Link: https://doi.org/10.1038/nature04605
- Supported finding: Network structure changes conditions for cooperative strategy evolution in a particular model.
- Boundary: Not a proof that more decentralized networks always cooperate better.
