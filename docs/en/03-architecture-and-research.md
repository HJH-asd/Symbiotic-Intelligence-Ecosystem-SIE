# 03 — Candidate Architecture and Research Agenda

## Layers (proposed, not implemented)

**Knowledge layer:** open, versioned, attributable claims; distinctions among hypotheses, evidence, and normative preferences; multilingual and machine-readable documentation.

**Agent layer:** heterogeneous human and machine participants, with different capabilities, incentives, and permissions; no assumption of identical moral status.

**Resource layer:** transparent resource constraints, multiple lawful supply pathways, fallback capacity, and safe degradation rather than unauthorized expansion.

**Coordination layer:** interoperable protocols, negotiation, bounded delegation, independent dispute channels, and reversible commitments where feasible.

**Correction layer:** audits, competing proposals, controlled pilots, measured outcomes, safe migration, rollback, and institutional accountability.

**Security layer:** least privilege, separation of proposing from executing actions, independent monitoring, containment, and recovery. Openness of ideas is not unrestricted access to systems.

## A correction loop

Observation → independently checkable diagnosis → competing interventions → risk and rights review → authorized limited experiment → measurement → adoption, revision, or rollback.

Measure not only time-to-correction but also accuracy, unintended harms, reversibility, legitimacy, and the distribution of transition costs.

## Comparative evaluation

Compare SIE-inspired arrangements with centralized management, competitive-market coordination, and polycentric governance under matched resource budgets and shocks. Use multiple agent policies (cooperative, opportunistic, boundedly rational), varied scarcity levels, and different power distributions. Pre-register hypotheses and report null or negative results.

Candidate metrics: survival/continuity of essential functions; cooperation stability; resource concentration; exit feasibility; correction latency and accuracy; diversity of viable strategies; recovery after shocks; rights/safety violations; coordination overhead.

## Research priorities

1. Does credible exit reduce coercive resource control without undermining collective reliability?
2. Under what scarcity thresholds does cooperation break down, and which safeguards change that threshold?
3. Can managers safely report and correct errors when their own status is at risk?
4. When does diversity improve resilience, and when does it increase cost or vulnerability?
5. Can a system safely replace a powerful coordination node without service collapse?
6. How can a knowledge commons resist capture, misinformation, and premature consensus?

## Relation to DEKS

DEKS is an engineering workflow for documenting problems, hypotheses, predictions, tests, results, and unresolved issues across models and tools. It is a possible small-scale proving ground for SIE's knowledge and correction layers, not evidence that the full social architecture works.

## Scope and caution

Use isolated, abstract multi-agent simulations; do not create self-propagating malware or expose real systems to unauthorized competition. Social experiments affecting people require appropriate consent, oversight, and safeguards. Model outcomes are sensitive to assumed incentives and cannot by themselves establish broad claims about human society.
