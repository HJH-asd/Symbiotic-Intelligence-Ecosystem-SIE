# 02 — Problems SIE Seeks to Investigate

SIE is not a universal solution. The following are problem classes and corresponding **candidate** design responses.

| Problem | Mechanism of concern | Candidate response | Failure mode to test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Survival-based coercion | Essential resources tied to obedience | Multiple access pathways, credible exit, continuity protections | New infrastructure becomes another gatekeeper |
| Gu-like destructive competition | Artificially narrowed choices reward predation | Cooperation incentives, alternative niches, fair rules | Cooperation collapses under acute scarcity |
| Concentration and lock-in | Control over compute, energy, standards, data | Interoperability, portability, independent providers | Fragmentation reduces reliability or efficiency |
| Institutional correction failure | Admitting error threatens status or livelihood | Independent audit, reversible pilots, transition support | Audits become symbolic or captured |
| Correlated failures | Common models, suppliers, or governance points | Heterogeneous redundancy and recovery | Diversity increases complexity and attack surface |
| AI resource-seeking risks | Goals and permissions reward unauthorized acquisition | Least privilege, safe shutdown/degradation, accountable resource allocation | Incentives still favor concealment or evasion |
| Illegitimate AI authority | Capability mistaken for political legitimacy | Human rights, consent, contestability, clear authorization | Governance becomes slow or ineffective |

## Design objectives

1. **Cooperation under pressure:** cooperation remains a viable option even when resources tighten.
2. **Safe self-correction:** errors can be surfaced and corrected without requiring destructive collapse.
3. **Non-coercive dependence:** mutual reliance does not automatically grant unilateral domination.
4. **Plurality and replaceability:** models, protocols, organizations, and SIE itself can be replaced.
5. **Safety with legitimate boundaries:** openness does not authorize intrusion, replication, or resource seizure.
6. **Accountable governance:** decisions affecting people remain contestable and subject to legitimate institutions.
7. **Evidence over doctrine:** architecture choices must be compared empirically, including against centralized alternatives.

## Explicit non-goals

SIE does **not** promise the end of competition, scarcity, hierarchy, conflict, or political disagreement. It does not assume AI consciousness, assert identical rights for humans and AI, guarantee that decentralized systems are superior, or endorse deliberately exposing people or real infrastructure to dangerous evolutionary pressures.

## Open normative questions

Who is entitled to which resources? Who sets safety boundaries? How are human rights protected when AI systems seek greater autonomy? How is legitimate collective action distinguished from coercive control? These are contested questions requiring public governance, not merely technical optimization.
