# 08 — Must a Cross-Intelligence Ecosystem Use Money or Points?

**Status:** Open research questions, not a recommendation to abolish currency.

## Separate the functions
Accounting for joules, compute-hours, materials and emissions is **not** the same as using a transferable monetary asset. Allocation, exchange, incentives, saving, reputation, dispute settlement and basic access can be implemented through different mechanisms.

## Candidate regimes (comparative baselines)
| Regime | Coordination benefit | Failure mode to test |
|---|---|---|
| Transferable money / prices | Distributed demand signalling, exchange, intertemporal claims | Wealth concentration, exclusion, externalities |
| Contribution points / reputation | Auditable contribution and local incentives | Score gaming, metric capture, cumulative advantage |
| Physical budgets / quotas | Direct relation to resource constraints | Rigidity, allocation capture, hidden opportunity costs |
| Mutual credit / bilateral reciprocity | Local agreements without a global currency | Trust fragmentation, clearing and enforcement problems |
| Needs-based guarantees + mixed allocation | Basic access protected; discretionary resources negotiated | Governance capture, financing, boundary disputes |
| Central planning | Potentially coherent resource prioritization | Information bottlenecks, concentrated authority |

**No single regime is presumed superior.** Many real systems will combine these methods. Reputation should not automatically become purchasing power, and ecological integrity should not be reduced to a market price alone.

## Distinct resource rights
1. **Use**: consume bounded resources for an authorized task.
2. **Request/negotiate**: express needs and participate in allocation.
3. **Control/accumulate/transfer**: hold durable claims, including exclusion powers.

These are distinct institutional choices. AI autonomy does not automatically imply unrestricted property rights or independent claims over essential human and ecological resources. Conversely, a token balance does not guarantee autonomy if one infrastructure owner can arbitrarily cut access.

## Cross-ecological constraints
Include energy, water, land, material extraction, emissions, biodiversity and habitat indicators alongside task throughput. Some impacts are non-fungible or irreversible; model hard limits and procedural representation rather than assuming all losses can be compensated by tokens.

## Testable predictions (not facts)
- H-E1: Under severe scarcity, transferable-credit-only regimes may concentrate access more than mixed regimes with protected minimum access.
- H-E2: Pure points-based incentives may induce score optimization that degrades ecological outcomes when those outcomes are omitted from the score.
- H-E3: Local reciprocity may preserve pluralism but incur higher coordination costs as interoperability declines.
- H-E4: A mixed regime may outperform both fully centralized and fully market-based baselines on some resilience metrics, but not universally.

Use identical resource budgets, agents, environmental dynamics and shocks; measure concentration, unmet essential needs, transaction overhead, reversibility of decisions, ecological damage, recovery and ability to exit a provider. See [experimental protocol](../../research/experiment-protocol.md).
