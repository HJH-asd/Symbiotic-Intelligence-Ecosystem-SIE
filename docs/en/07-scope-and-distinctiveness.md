# 07 — Scope, Distinctiveness, and Related Concepts

**Status:** Research positioning; differences are hypotheses about scope, not claims of priority or uniqueness. **Version:** 0.4.

## Definition
The **Symbiotic Intelligence Ecosystem (SIE)** is a proposed research framework for interactions and co-evolution among human societies, AI systems, robots, other living organisms, natural ecosystems, and the material/technical infrastructures that connect them. Its unit of inquiry is **relations among partly autonomous systems**, not a single network of software agents.

Natural ecosystems are not merely background scenery, an inventory of resources, or an analogy. Biodiversity, habitat integrity, regeneration, biogeochemical cycles and irreversible ecological damage are part of the studied system. Not all organisms or ecosystems need to be classified as intelligent agents to be relevant.

## Comparison: overlap without equivalence
| Existing concept / literature | What it typically studies | What SIE adds as a proposed research focus | Relationship |
|---|---|---|---|
| Multi-agent systems (MAS), multi-agent RL | Interacting agents, policies, incentives, task coordination | Cross-organizational and cross-biophysical dependencies; institutions and natural ecosystems | Subsystem / experimental method |
| Human–AI symbiosis and collective alignment | Human–AI collaboration, cognitive coordination, value differences | Physical resource control, survival dependency, institutional lock-in, ecological consequences | Complementary mechanism |
| AI/agent ecosystems and platform ecology | Agent marketplaces, protocols, platform incentives, business networks | No mandatory global protocol, currency, ownership model or platform operator | Candidate local architectures |
| Social–ecological systems (SES), commons governance | Institutions, communities, resources and ecosystem dynamics | AI and autonomous robotics as new adaptive participants in coupled systems | Major theoretical foundation, not a replacement |
| Earth-system science and ecological economics | Planetary processes, material throughput, environmental constraints | Interactions with autonomous AI infrastructures and institutional feedback | Major theoretical foundation |
| Cybernetics, complex adaptive systems, resilience | Feedback, adaptation, networked failure, system viability | Comparative cross-intelligence ecological governance and self-correction | Shared methods |

**Novelty boundary:** The terms *symbiosis*, *intelligence ecosystem*, and even *symbiotic intelligence ecosystem* have appeared in other contexts. SIE makes **no first-use claim**. A possible contribution is the particular integrated set of questions and falsifiable comparisons; this remains to be demonstrated against prior literature.

## Not the same as a larger MAS
1. Participants do not necessarily share an ontology, protocol, currency, owner, or utility function.
2. Some affected systems (wetlands, watersheds, microbial communities) cannot bargain or issue tokens; their effects and protections still matter.
3. Coordination may be local, incompatible, contested, or absent; governance arrangements themselves evolve.
4. Material and ecological limits are endogenous to the model rather than unlimited external supplies.
5. Outcomes are assessed at multiple scales and across time, not solely by agent task performance.

## Questions that distinguish the agenda
- When does locally beneficial cooperation cause harms to third parties or ecosystems?
- How do control of compute, energy, land, minerals, data and manufacturing shape autonomy and exit options?
- When does a governing institution become unable or unwilling to correct itself because its survival depends on existing arrangements?
- Can heterogeneous governance regimes coexist without converging on an indispensable central coordinator?
- Which arrangements preserve ecological integrity and long-term diversity under scarcity and shocks?
- Are fungible money, tokens, or reputation points necessary, beneficial, or harmful under different conditions?

## Interpretation rules
SIE is **not** a completed political constitution, a universally optimal design, a claim that all natural systems are conscious, or a mandate to grant unrestricted resource rights to AI. Competing architectures—including central coordination and markets—are legitimate baselines. Negative findings and replacement of SIE are acceptable outcomes.
